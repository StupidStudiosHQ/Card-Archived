# CardArchived Interest (CAI)
## Internal Acquisition & Research Strategy — v1.0

**INTERNAL PROGRAM — NOT A PUBLIC PRODUCT OR CUSTOMER ENTITLEMENT**

## Purpose

CardArchived Interest (CAI) is an internal escalation path for objects encountered through Quick Look or another CardArchived workflow that appear unusually important, visually compelling, materially unusual, historically interesting, technically informative, or otherwise valuable to the development of the archive.

CAI allows CardArchived to selectively subsidize deeper examination of objects that the company itself wants to study.

## Core Principle

A customer may purchase a low-cost Quick Look and receive the ordinary Quick Look result. Separately, the review system may flag the object internally for CAI review.

A CAI flag is not an Artistic Presentation grade, a prediction of Exceptional status, an authentication conclusion, a valuation, or a guarantee of any future classification.

It means only: **CardArchived believes this object may be worth studying more deeply.**

## Internal Trigger

Potential trigger label:

**CAI REVIEW — ESCALATE TO JAY**

Possible reasons include:

- extraordinary or unusual visual execution;
- unusual materials, construction, printing, inserts, film, relics, or manufacturing characteristics;
- historically or culturally important subject matter;
- unidentified or under-documented creative work;
- unusual or potentially significant provenance;
- especially useful specimen-identification characteristics;
- an object category or production technique not previously represented in the archive;
- an unexpected research question raised by the object;
- an object that is simply compelling enough that CardArchived wants to understand it.

## Approval

Automated systems or reviewers may flag an object for CAI review. The decision to make a subsidized examination offer is a separate internal decision.

The customer cannot purchase CAI status, apply for it, request it as an upgrade, or select it during checkout.

## Customer Contact

If CardArchived elects to pursue the object, CardArchived may contact the submitter personally and explain that the company would like to examine the object more deeply.

Suggested communication:

> I reviewed the object you submitted to CardArchived and we'd like the opportunity to examine it more closely. There's something about this piece that we believe warrants a deeper physical archive and research process. We'd like to offer that evaluation at our direct processing cost. If you're interested, contact me personally and I'll explain what we'd like to examine and why.

## Pricing Principle

The normal objective is **break-even, not free**.

The CAI price is based on CardArchived's measured direct processing cost for the requested examination. It is not a permanent advertised price and should be recalibrated as actual labor, research, AI/computation, payment, storage, handling, shipping/insurance (if applicable), and other marginal costs become known.

CardArchived may elect to absorb some or all cost in exceptional research circumstances, but free evaluation is not the default.

## Separation From Artistic Presentation

CAI must never become a backdoor route to Exceptional or Gold.

Selection for deeper study means **interesting**, not **artistically superior**.

The Artistic Presentation methodology remains independent:

- Internal 1–4 → Public: Below Common
- Internal 5–8 → Public: Standard
- Internal 9 → Public: Exceptional
- Internal 10 → Public: Exceptional, with eventual Gold Plate treatment

The internal numerical assessment is not publicly displayed. Gold cannot be purchased, requested, or awarded because an object was selected for CAI.

## Strategic Value

CAI gives CardArchived a controlled mechanism to curate unusually informative records without turning curation into a public pay-to-play service.

Over time, CAI may help the archive develop unusually deep records around rare production techniques, important creators, unusual materials, historically significant objects, emerging counterfeit patterns, novel specimen fingerprints, and collectible categories CardArchived did not originally anticipate.

The program can therefore serve simultaneously as:

1. an internal research pipeline;
2. an archive-curation mechanism;
3. an early-warning system for unexpected object types and risks;
4. a discovery mechanism for future products, methodologies, partnerships, or research priorities.

## Confidentiality / Public Positioning

CAI is not advertised as a guaranteed benefit of Quick Look. Public Quick Look materials should not promise or imply that a customer may receive a subsidized deeper evaluation.

If collectors eventually become aware that CardArchived occasionally initiates deeper research offers, that does not create an entitlement to consideration or selection.

## Guardrails

- Never imply CAI selection guarantees authenticity, identity, value, rarity, Exceptional status, or Gold treatment.
- Never change a frozen specimen fingerprint to accommodate a CAI object.
- Never invent creative attribution to make an object appear more significant.
- Keep research interest separate from ownership claims.
- Record why an object was escalated internally so the program can later be audited for bias or drift.
- Measure actual subsidized processing cost so CAI does not quietly become an uncontrolled loss center.
